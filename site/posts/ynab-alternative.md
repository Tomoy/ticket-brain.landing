YNAB, Copilot and Monarch are good budgeting apps. If you want a complete
picture of your finances — accounts, investments, net worth, goals — they do
that well and TicketBrain does not try to.

But there is a specific place where they run out of resolution, and it happens
to be the biggest variable expense in most households.

## Your bank sees a total, not a shop

Budgeting apps are built on bank data. When you shop, your bank reports one
line:

> SUPERMARKET · €87.40

Your budgeting app files that under Groceries. It is accurate and completely
unactionable. You know you spent €87.40. You do not know that €23 of it was
snacks you did not plan to buy, or that the same four items have quietly gone
up 15% since spring.

When your grocery category runs over month after month, the app can tell you
*that* it happened. It cannot tell you *why*.

## The receipt has the resolution the bank feed lacks

The receipt in your pocket lists every item and every price. That is the level
where grocery decisions actually get made.

With item-level data you can see which categories are growing, catch the
repeat purchases that add up invisibly, watch how prices move on the things
you buy most, and get recommendations grounded in what you really bought.

Side by side, the two tools are answering different questions:

| | Budgeting apps (YNAB, Copilot, Monarch) | TicketBrain |
|---|---|---|
| Where the data comes from | Your bank feed | The receipt itself |
| What you see for a grocery shop | One line: SUPERMARKET €87.40 | Every item, its price and its category |
| Covers all your accounts | Yes | No, groceries only |
| Net worth, investments, savings goals | Yes | No |
| Answers "why did groceries go up?" | No | Yes |
| Needs a bank connection | Yes | No |
| Effort per shop | None, it syncs | One photo of the receipt |

## Use both

This is not really an either/or. A budgeting app is the right tool for your
overall finances. TicketBrain goes deep on the one category that is hardest to
control and easiest to overspend on.

If groceries are the line that keeps breaking your budget, that is the gap we
are built to close.
