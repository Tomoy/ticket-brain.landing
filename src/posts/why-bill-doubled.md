If you’ve felt like your grocery bill **skyrocketed over the last few years**, you’re not imagining things. Prices *have* gone up — but not always for the reasons we assume.  

There are three major forces at play:  

## 1. Inflation
Basic inflation has affected nearly every product on the shelf. But it hasn’t hit categories equally:  
- **Meat, dairy, and eggs** saw some of the sharpest increases between 2021–2023.  
- **Staples** like rice and pasta rose, but less aggressively.  
- **Snacks and convenience foods** often increased above average, fueled by demand.  

📊 Data from consumer reports shows that between 2019–2024, average food inflation across major economies ranged from **25%–40%**, depending on category.  

## 2. Shrinkflation
Sometimes the price doesn’t change — but the package does.  
- A 500g cereal box quietly becomes 420g.  
- Yogurt packs lose a cup, while the shelf price stays the same.  

This “invisible inflation” has hit shoppers hard because it’s harder to notice.  

## 3. Behavioral Changes
Here’s the tricky part: not all increases come from the store. Sometimes, it’s us:  
- Adding more snacks and treats to the basket.  
- Switching from generic to branded products.  
- Shopping more frequently (leading to more impulse buys).  

---

## What You *Can* Control

You can’t reverse global inflation, but you **can** influence your own basket:  
- **Spot shrinkflation:** Compare unit prices, not just shelf prices.  
- **Audit your habits:** Look for categories where your own behavior shifted (e.g., buying branded instead of generic).  
- **Plan smarter trips:** Fewer shopping trips mean fewer unplanned extras.  

---

## The Bottom Line
Your grocery bill doubled because of a mix of global forces and small, unnoticed personal choices.  
The good news? **You can’t control inflation, but you *can* control your habits.**  
And often, that’s where the biggest savings hide.
